## Numbers

Uploading 256 MiB through `api` to RustFS, MB/s, median of 5. Release build; the setup is in
D-0081.

| Part size | Single `PUT` | Parts ×1 | Parts ×4 | tus `PATCH` | tus per part |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 MiB | 255 | 79 | 288 | 83 | 58 |
| 16 MiB | 376 | 157 | 508 | 144 | 156 |
| 64 MiB | 297 | 228 | 731 | 223 | 192 |

The decision: parallel parts of 16 MiB, and tus is removed.

The absolute numbers include podman's WSL 2 port forwarding and RustFS running on a 4-vCPU VM.
M3's benchmark measures latency and concurrency against the same stack, and Prompt 13 measures
on the production hardware.

## Changes from the plan

- **tus is gone** after the measurement, as the plan said the loser would be.
- **The client side** of resumable uploads, for tests, is `songbird_api::client::ApiClient`.
  `crates/client` speaks the WebSocket protocol only, and apps implement uploads from
  PROTOCOL §7.2.
- **The worker's abort sweep has no test of its own yet.** It runs as part of
  `Worker::sweep`. The lifecycle gate test in M3 (`gate_p06`) covers it end to end.

## Owner decisions in this milestone

- Threat model §1, two rows awaiting approval:
  - resumable grants keep the device next to the object ID for up to 24 h instead of 1 h;
  - the keyed budget records.

## Open risks

- The budgets are per account. A client with many accounts multiplies them, which registration
  (invite-only, proof of work) already bounds. Plans and abuse limits come in Prompt 11.
- Parts lists are capped at 1,000. At 16 MiB that is 16 GiB, far above the 2 GiB limit, and the
  config refuses part sizes that would need more than 10,000 parts.
