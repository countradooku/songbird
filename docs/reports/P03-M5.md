## What was built

1. **Encrypted profiles** (`crates/proto/src/profile.rs`, `services/api/src/profiles.rs`,
   PROTOCOL.md §4.8). One opaque blob encrypted under the profile key, at most 8 KiB: the name,
   avatar pointer, about, status line and profile links, laid out by the client. Versions only
   grow; fetches are throttled per requester; pending devices may not change it.
2. **Encrypted avatars** in `RustFS`, at most 512 KiB, under random IDs:
   - only the account's current avatar is served;
   - replaced ones are swept by the worker after an hour;
   - all of them are deleted, and verified gone, with the account.
3. **Unidentified-access key** (`songbird_keys::sealed`), derived from the profile key with
   HKDF, stored with the profile, with an "anyone may send" flag.
4. **Sealed sends** (`PUT /v1/messages/sealed`, off by default):
   - no device signature; authorized by the XOR of the closed recipient accounts' access keys,
     compared in constant time;
   - the device-list check still applies;
   - throttled per IP range and per recipient account.
5. **Sender certificates** (`GET /v1/certificate`): Ed25519 by a dedicated certificate key,
   binding the account, device, device identity key and a 24-hour expiry; the vector is
   cross-checked with OpenSSL.
6. Docs: PROTOCOL.md §4.8 and §13 (certificate vector); threat model §1 (two rows, awaiting
   approval), §3.14 and open risks; D-0054; `[api.profiles]`; fuzz target `profile`, in the
   nightly matrix.

## Open risks

- The server still sees the sealed sender's IP address and timing; the proxy or Tor hides them.
- Revoking a contact's sealed access means rotating the profile key.
- The certificate key is not deployed yet: it goes in the secrets store, rotated by `key_id`.
- Prompt 12 verifies sealed sender before the flag goes on.
