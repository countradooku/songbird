The capture showed that LiveKit's `room_started` webhook carries the room's TURN password; the
receiver never stores or logs bodies, and the endpoint is loopback only.

## Decisions (D-0088)

- **Links live in SurrealDB, lobbies in `valkey-hot`.** A link must survive restarts for days;
  `valkey-hot` has no persistence. A lobby is minutes long and needs no durability.
- **The admin is a token, not an account**, as for packs: the server never learns who made a link.
  The price: deleting an account cannot find its links; they go at their expiry (≤ 30 days).
- **Lobby places are bound to a keyed tag of the waiting device**, so a co-admin who sees the
  handles cannot redeem someone else's place for another device.
- **`auto_create: false`** in LiveKit (M4 writes it into every configuration), so tokens admit only
  to rooms `api` created with their caps; `ListParticipants` answers an empty list for a missing
  room, so liveness uses `ListRooms`.
- **Webhooks feed counters, not gauges**: a restart cannot leave a negative gauge; dashboards take
  started minus finished.

## Changes from the plan

- The fuzz target is `call_records` (the binary decoders of what `valkey-hot` holds), not
  `call_link`: link requests are JSON through `serde` with `deny_unknown_fields`.
- The plan's "records gone after `room_finished`" is shown with signed events built exactly as
  LiveKit builds them, plus one replayed real LiveKit webhook; CI's LiveKit (compose) cannot reach
  the test process, so the real-webhook test is opt-in.

## Open risks

- Link creators who lose the admin token cannot revoke; the link expires on its own.
- A lobby poll every few seconds costs one Valkey round trip each; at the budget (1,800 an hour per
  device) that is negligible.
