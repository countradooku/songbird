## Decisions (D-0087)

- **Group calls are one per group**, claimed with `SET NX` so two starters meet in one room; any
  member joins a live call, starting one needs the policy's `call` role. Read-only members
  (channels) get tickets that publish nothing; a group's audio call publishes the microphone only,
  so its larger cap (128) is not spent on video.
- **Removal is a reconcile, not a per-device table**: after any accepted commit to a group with a
  live call, `api` lists the room's identities and disconnects every one that is not a roster
  device's. Identities are recomputable HMACs, so nothing maps them back to devices. It also
  covers devices that left by `SelfRemove` and the delivery service's removals (D-0075), which
  reach the group through ordinary commits.
- **The callee is proven a contact from the directory snapshot** (its access key, compared in
  constant time, or the open flag), as the send path's lanes do; an open account that blocked the
  caller gets the same answer and no wake-up.
- **Tokens use `livekit-api`'s `access-token` feature** (HS256 with its HMAC-only provider);
  server-API calls use our Twirp client on hyper and rustls with aws-lc-rs.

## Changes from the plan

- The record names, budgets and identity derivation key off one 32-byte calls secret through
  HMAC sub-keys (`songbird.v1/call-identity`, `songbird.v1/call-records`), like the media and GIF
  keys.
- The plan said the M2 gate would show a removed member is disconnected; it is shown with a
  signalling-only participant (LiveKit's WebSocket), since media needs libwebrtc and belongs to the
  M4 harness. The harness repeats it with media and E2EE.

## Open risks

- `livekit-token` copies the API secret into a plain `String` per token (not zeroized); the
  secret is long-lived in the process anyway.
- A call longer than 24 hours keeps its room but its record expires, so nobody new can join it.
- Wake-ups for group calls go to every member's devices (up to 32 accounts); Prompt 8 decides the
  push payloads and collapses.
