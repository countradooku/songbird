## What was built

1. **The `Send` frame's hints** (`crates/proto/src/frame.rs`, PROTOCOL §1.2):
   - four optional blocks behind new flags: `ACCESS` (16-byte combined access key), `REQUEST`,
     `FRANKED` (32-byte commitment) and `EXPIRES` (seconds, 1 to 30 days);
   - flag rules: access or request, never both; neither on a shared body; a request is never an
     MLS commit;
   - unknown bits stay refused, and each block appears only with its flag, so every frame keeps one
     encoding;
   - reject reasons 10 (access required) and 11 (invalid request) are defined, and enforced from
     M4.
2. **Franking stamps** (`crates/proto/src/envelope.rs` for the format, `crates/send/src/franking.rs`
   for the MAC):
   - every envelope of a franked send for another account is stored as `stamp ‖ envelope`;
   - the stamp's MAC covers the key ID, commitment, server time, sender account and recipient
     account. A sealed sender is zeros; a shared body is stamped once, for no recipient;
   - the sender's own devices are not stamped;
   - delivery routes exactly what the mailbox stored.
3. **The reserved prefix** `F5 53 42`: every format the server writes into an envelope (system
   frames, stamps, anything later) is refused from users with reason 9.
4. **Disappearing envelopes**: `StoreRequest::expires_after` in all three mailboxes. A send's
   envelopes are never fetched after the deadline (their `seq` skipped), and the sweep deletes
   them even if no device connected. Iggy:
   - deadlines in the sealed store metadata and relocated slots (format 5, with 3 and 4 still
     read);
   - carried over node links (wire version 2) and by the re-driver;
   - expired entries dropped by compaction wherever they are in a device's queue, their keys
     punctured, so a restart cannot bring them back.
5. **Keys and configuration**: `[delivery] franking_key` and `[api.send] franking_key`, plus
   `previous_franking_keys` for M5's reports, are required in staging and production. Without a
   key, a franked send is refused as unavailable.
6. Docs:
   - PROTOCOL.md §1.2 (flags, reasons 9 to 11), §5.13 and §13 (frame vectors, the stamp's known
     answer);
   - threat model §1 (two rows, awaiting approval) and §3.19;
   - D-0061;
   - fuzz target `franking`, in the nightly matrix.

## Open risks

- **Iggy was not run locally.** The podman VM ran out of memory with Iggy in Prompt 3, so CI's
  compose job runs the Iggy live tests, the new one included.
- **Sealed senders are not attributable** in a report: the stamp proves the content and the
  delivery, not the sender. Requests are never sealed (M4).
- **A holder of the franking key and the database could test candidate senders** against an
  envelope that is still undelivered. Once acknowledged, the envelope is sealed under a destroyed
  key. This is the first of the two §1 rows.
- **Stamped envelopes cost one allocation each**, for franked sends only. The M3 allocation gate
  measures unfranked sends and is unchanged; the full suite confirms it before the gate.

## For the owner

Two threat-model §1 rows (awaiting approval):

1. **A disappearing envelope's deadline**, stored with it.
2. **Franking stamps**: a MAC binding commitment, time, sender and recipient accounts, with
   neither account in the clear, stored with each franked envelope until delivery.
