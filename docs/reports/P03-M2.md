## What was built

1. **Usernames as hashes with proofs** (`crates/keys/src/username.rs`). One person per name,
   3 to 32 ASCII letters, digits, `_`, `.`, starting with a letter, unique in lower case. The
   server stores `s·G` on Ristretto255 (`s` from SHA-512 of the domain-separated lower-cased
   name, `G` squeezed from libsignal's SHO under a Songbird label) and requires a **poksho proof**
   that the claimant knows `s`.
2. **Proven against libsignal** without linking it (AGPL, D-0047): the implementation first
   reproduced libsignal's own username hashes and proofs exactly (12 of 12, nonce derivation
   included); after the change to unique handles, libsignal's poksho produces the same
   generator, hash and proof bytes for Songbird's statement and verifies Songbird's proof.
   `tools/libsignal-vectors` (outside the workspace, never built by CI) does it by hand.
3. **Registry** (`crates/accounts`): `username` (hash → account; held 30 days after a release,
   naming nobody, then swept), `account.username`, `account.ukey`, `ulink` (random handle →
   account and ciphertext). Claims in one transaction (racing claims conflict and retry).
   Account deletion holds the name and deletes the link; the worker's verification counts both.
4. **`api` endpoints** (`services/api/src/usernames.rs`, PROTOCOL.md §4.3): claim, release,
   exact-match lookup (hash in a signed POST body, never a URL), username key, links. Reserved
   names refused by hash.
5. **The username key**: 4 to 10 digits; `salt || Argon2id(19 MiB, t = 2, secret = pepper)`; a
   missing key, a wrong key and a spent guessing budget all answer 404 like an unknown name; a
   keyed lookup always runs one Argon2id check and touches the name's budget, an unkeyed one
   never does.
6. **Throttles** (GCRA in `valkey-hot`, keyed, fail closed): lookups per account and per IP
   range; claims per account, every attempt counted; key guesses per username across all
   requesters.
7. Worker sweep of ended holds; fuzz target `username`; docs: PROTOCOL.md §4.3 and §13 vector,
   threat model §1 (four rows, awaiting the owner) and §3.9, runbook "Usernames: lookups or
   claims failing", D-0049, `config/dev/api.toml`.

## Numbers

- Keyed lookup at the production Argon2id cost (19 MiB, t = 2; debug build, so slower than
  release): mean 0.1916 s for an existing name with a wrong key, 0.1926 s for an absent name;
  ratio 0.995 (`keyed_lookups_take_the_same_time_whether_or_not_the_username_exists`).
- Proof verification: two scalar multiplications and one SHO transcript per claim; not on any
  hot path.

## Security review

`security-review` over the diff (an independent reviewer, read-only): **no finding at or above
the reporting bar.** It checked SurrealQL injection (every value bound), authorization (the
account always comes from the signed device), claim rules (held and reserved names, races, the
proof), the cryptography (canonical encodings, the identity point, transcript binding, nonce
derivation, constant-time verifier comparison), enumeration (answers, and the database and Valkey
round trips on every not-found path), data exposure and deletion. Of its notes below the bar,
two were cheap to close and are fixed:

- **The key-guessing budget was checked and spent in two calls**, so concurrent wrong guesses
  could share one token. Now one atomic Lua step (`KEY_GUESS`): a spent budget refuses, a lookup
  that finds nothing spends one guess, one round trip on every path.
- **A username stayed findable while its account was being deleted.** Lookups now treat an
  account marked for deletion as not found (same statement, same work); tested.

The others are by design and recorded: a claim answers `taken` for a name that exists (inherent
to unique names; claims are throttled and every attempt counts); proofs are not bound to an
account (harmless: knowing the name is enough to make one); anyone can spend a name's key budget
(a deliberate trade-off, listed under open risks).

## Open risks

- **Usernames are guessable from their hashes.** The hash cannot be salted (clients compute it
  from the name alone), so a database thief or a compromised server finds common names by
  trying them. Recorded in the threat model; inherent to Signal's construction too.
- Spending someone's key-guessing budget makes their name unfindable with the key for up to
  about ten hours (links and QR codes still work).
- Name disputes and impersonation by people need an abuse process (Prompt 11); reserved names
  only cover the service's own.
- The username key's pepper sits in `api`'s secrets: a compromised `api` can test keys offline.
