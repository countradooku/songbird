## What was built

- **Valkey router** (`crates/router/src/valkey.rs`): one channel per device, the subscriptions
  being the presence registry; a new connection publishes `close` on its channel, then
  subscribes, then drains. Valkey sees no identifiers and no content: channel names are keyed
  hashes of the device ID, payloads sealed (AES-256-GCM-SIV, bound to channel and kind), stale
  `close` messages ignored. Subscriber connections pinged and replaced when silent; everything
  re-subscribed after a reconnect, local sessions told to drain.
- **Self-healing Valkey connections** (`crates/router/src/conn.rs`) for publishes and leases.
- **Node links** (`crates/link`): framed, multiplexed request/response over TCP with mutual TLS
  from the internal CA; only peers whose certificate is valid for a `delivery.` name they claim
  are served.
- **Mailbox cluster mode** (`crates/mailbox/src/iggy/{cluster,partitions,wire}.rs`): each
  partition has one owner node (a lease in Valkey) and one standby (rendezvous hashing); every
  per-partition operation (store, fetch, ACK, count, part completion) goes to the owner over node
  links; the owner copies its key store to the standby (whole when the standby changes, then
  every write). A dead owner's partitions go to the standby after a lease period; a graceful
  shutdown hands them over at once; leases Valkey lost are taken back without restarting
  anything; Iggy's producer epochs still fence a stale writer.
- **Delivery**: `[delivery.router] backend = "valkey"`, `[delivery.cluster]`, Valkey client
  certificates, graceful mailbox shutdown on SIGTERM.
- **Load generator**: several `--addr`, devices spread over them.
- **Sockudo's Redis Cluster lessons** (sockudo/sockudo#510, pointed out by the owner) adopted:
  pinged subscribers, no hot channel, phased layout migrations, per-shard subscribers for a
  clustered Valkey (D-0034).

## Cluster run (`bench/m4/cluster.sh`)

Run 5 (the final code; runs 3 and 4 gave the same steady numbers within noise). Three nodes,
3,000 devices spread evenly over them, so two of every three messages cross nodes; four mailbox
partitions; lease period 3 s; every phase checked by the M1 checker (each message received
exactly once, in order).

| Phase | Rate | p50 | p99 | p99.9 | Checker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Steady | 5,000/s | 0.31 ms | 1.08 ms | 4.9 ms | PASS, 0 lost |
| Steady | 20,000/s | 0.48 ms | 1.52 ms | 3.7 ms | PASS, 0 lost |
| Steady | 50,000/s | 0.78 ms | 3.42 ms | 14.4 ms | PASS, 0 lost |
| Partition owner `kill -9` at 6 s, restarted at 14 s | 10,000/s | 0.39 ms | 7.0 s | 11.4 s | PASS, 0 lost |
| Valkey restarted at 6 s (no persistence) | 10,000/s | 0.38 ms | 2.5 ms | 128 ms | PASS, 0 lost |
| Partition owner SIGTERM at 6 s, restarted at 14 s | 10,000/s | 0.39 ms | 9.2 s | 11.5 s | PASS, 0 lost |

- **Owner killed**: its partitions were running again on the other two nodes 6 s after the
  kill (the lease expiring, then one more lease period in case Valkey had lost a live owner's
  lease); no partition failed to start, the standbys had every key-store file.
- **Valkey restarted**: no partition changed hands or restarted; every lease was taken back by
  its owner and every subscription renewed. The p99.9 of 128 ms is the restart itself.
- **Graceful stop**: the standbys took the partitions 2–3 s after SIGTERM (the stopping node
  first writes and copies its key stores).
- No replication warning during the measured phases (one during the script's final teardown,
  when the standby was already gone; the store is then copied whole on the next write).

The kill and graceful phases target the node holding the partitions (read from the leases).
While a node is down its 1,000 devices cannot connect (the load generator pins each device to
one node; a load balancer would send them elsewhere), so messages to them wait in the mailbox
until it is back: that is the multi-second p99 of those phases, not a delay of the cluster.

## Bugs found and fixed (all by the tests and runs above)

- **Valkey connections never healed after a Valkey restart** (chaos run 2 hung for an hour):
  leases stopped renewing and expired, a restarted node took the partitions while the others
  still ran them, and routed publishes went nowhere. Now self-healing connections, with tests
  that drop every Valkey connection mid-run.
- **A lease lost with Valkey restarted a running partition**, and a renewal racing a lost key
  stopped a running writer: now taken back without a restart (`leases_lost_with_valkey_are_
  taken_back_without_restarting`, which fails without the fix: 8 partition starts instead of 4).
- **A standby that joined late lacked the key-store files written before it arrived**: the
  owner now copies the whole store when its standby changes.
- **A non-standby node raced the standby for a dead owner's partitions**: correct (it refused
  for want of keys and released) but slow; the standby now has first claim.
- **Graceful handovers waited a lease period**: a `released` marker lets the standby take the
  partitions at once.
- **Expiry missed entries stored in the same millisecond** (latent since M2; showed when the
  send path got faster): the age bound is now inclusive.

## Open risks and follow-ups

1. **Sticky ownership**: a partition stays with its owner while it lives, so a node joining later
   takes no load until a partition becomes free; live rebalancing (handover between two live
   nodes) is follow-up work.
2. **Failover after a crash takes two lease periods** (6 s at the 3 s used here; the lease can
   go down to 1 s). The second period protects against needless handovers when Valkey loses a
   live owner's lease; telling the two cases apart (the members set) would halve it.
3. **A standby down when its owner dies**: the partition refuses to start elsewhere (no key store
   copy) until the standby returns; one standby per partition, a second is possible.
4. **Valkey without persistence** holds leases and routing: its loss costs a few seconds of
   re-subscribing and lease reclaiming, measured above.
5. **Clustered Valkey** (`SPUBLISH`/`SSUBSCRIBE`, per-shard subscribers) is designed, not built.
6. The single-machine numbers need the reference hardware (Prompt 13).
