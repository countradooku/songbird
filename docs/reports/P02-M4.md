## What was built

- **Valkey router** (`crates/router/src/valkey.rs`): one channel per device, the subscriptions
  being the presence registry; a new connection publishes `close` on its channel, then
  subscribes, then drains. Valkey sees no identifiers and no content: channel names are keyed
  hashes of the device ID, payloads sealed (AES-256-GCM-SIV, bound to channel and kind), stale
  `close` messages ignored. Subscriber connections pinged and replaced when silent; everything
  re-subscribed after a reconnect, local sessions told to drain.
- **Self-healing Valkey connections** (`crates/router/src/conn.rs`) for publishes and leases.
- **Node links** (`crates/link`): framed, multiplexed request/response over TCP with mutual TLS
  from the internal CA; only peers whose certificate is valid for a `delivery.` name they claim
  are served.
- **Mailbox cluster mode** (`crates/mailbox/src/iggy/{cluster,partitions,wire}.rs`): each
  partition has one owner node (a lease in Valkey) and **two standbys**, the second in another
  zone (`[delivery.cluster] zone`); every per-partition operation (store, fetch, ACK, count,
  part completion) goes to the owner over node links.
  - **A key window seals records only once a standby holds its keys**: a key on one disk only
    would die with it. With every standby unreachable, sends to that partition are refused
    (retryable) until one is back or drops out of the members.
  - A standby gets the whole store when it becomes one (or misses a write), then every write;
    a whole copy deletes the key files it no longer lists, and a node that is no longer a
    standby deletes its copy after three lease periods (deleted keys must not linger).
  - **Failover**: the old owner's first live standby takes the partition as soon as the lease
    and the owner's membership (expiring on Valkey's clock) are gone; one extra lease period
    only when the owner may be alive (still a member, or Valkey just lost everything, which a
    node detects when its own registration vanished).
  - **Rebalancing**: an owner hands a partition to the node ranked first for it once that node
    has been up two lease periods and holds the whole store, one partition per round.
  - A graceful shutdown hands everything over at once; leases Valkey lost are taken back
    without restarting anything; Iggy's producer epochs still fence a stale writer.
  - Default lease 3 s (was 10 s).
- **Delivery**: `[delivery.router] backend = "valkey"`, `[delivery.cluster]`, Valkey client
  certificates, graceful mailbox shutdown on SIGTERM.
- **Load generator**: several `--addr`, devices spread over them.
- **Sockudo's Redis Cluster lessons** (sockudo/sockudo#510, pointed out by the owner) adopted:
  pinged subscribers, no hot channel, phased layout migrations, per-shard subscribers for a
  clustered Valkey (D-0034).

## Cluster run (`bench/m4/cluster.sh`)

Run 6, the final code. Three nodes, 3,000 devices spread evenly over them, so two of every
three messages cross nodes; twelve mailbox partitions; lease period 3 s; every phase checked by
the M1 checker (each message received exactly once, in order).

| Phase | Rate | p50 | p99 | p99.9 | Checker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Steady | 5,000/s | 0.30 ms | 0.74 ms | 3.0 ms | PASS, 0 lost |
| Steady | 20,000/s | 0.48 ms | 1.04 ms | 3.5 ms | PASS, 0 lost |
| Steady | 50,000/s | 0.77 ms | 1.99 ms | 3.8 ms | PASS, 0 lost |
| Partition owner `kill -9` at 6 s, restarted at 14 s | 10,000/s | 0.37 ms | 8.5 s | 11.5 s | PASS, 0 lost |
| Valkey restarted at 6 s (no persistence) | 10,000/s | 0.35 ms | 1.2 ms | 123 ms | PASS, 0 lost |
| Partition owner SIGTERM at 6 s, restarted at 14 s | 10,000/s | 0.37 ms | 9.4 s | 11.5 s | PASS, 0 lost |
| Two nodes `kill -9` at once, restarted 8 s later | 10,000/s | 0.38 ms | 9.6 s | 11.9 s | PASS, 0 lost |
| Rolling restart (each node SIGTERM and back, 12 s apart) | 10,000/s | 0.36 ms | 4.2 s | 6.2 s | PASS, 0 lost |

- **Owner killed**: its partitions ran on their standbys about 3 s after the kill (run 5: 6 s).
- **Two nodes killed at once**: the survivor held a copy of every partition and ran all twelve
  4.6 s after the kill. One start attempt was refused first, correctly: the dead nodes still
  counted as members, so the new window had no second copy yet.
- **Valkey restarted**: no partition changed hands or restarted.
- **Graceful stop**: standbys took the partitions about 3 s after SIGTERM (the node drains its
  client sessions first).
- **Rebalancing**: after the start the twelve partitions settled 5/5/2 (node A, up first, handed
  seven over within 6 s; 5/5/2 is where rendezvous hashing puts them for those three node IDs);
  after the rolling restart, with new IDs, 4/4/4. No partition moved back and forth.
- Replication warnings appeared only in the two seconds after a kill, while the dead node still
  counted as a member; none in steady phases, no key-store copy failed in the end.
- Steady p99 improved over run 5 (2.0 against 3.4 ms at 50,000/s) with twelve partitions
  instead of four: more writers batching in parallel.

The kill and graceful phases target the node holding the partitions (read from the leases).
While a node is down its 1,000 devices cannot connect (the load generator pins each device to
one node; a load balancer would send them elsewhere), so messages to them wait in the mailbox
until it is back: that is the multi-second p99 of those phases, not a delay of the cluster.

## Bugs found and fixed (all by the tests and runs above)

- **Valkey connections never healed after a Valkey restart** (chaos run 2 hung for an hour):
  leases stopped renewing and expired, a restarted node took the partitions while the others
  still ran them, and routed publishes went nowhere. Now self-healing connections, with tests
  that drop every Valkey connection mid-run.
- **A lease lost with Valkey restarted a running partition**, and a renewal racing a lost key
  stopped a running writer: now taken back without a restart (`leases_lost_with_valkey_are_
  taken_back_without_restarting`, which fails without the fix: 8 partition starts instead of 4).
- **A standby that joined late lacked the key-store files written before it arrived**: the
  owner now copies the whole store when its standby changes.
- **A non-standby node raced the standby for a dead owner's partitions**: correct (it refused
  for want of keys and released) but slow; the standby now has first claim.
- **Graceful handovers waited a lease period**: a `released` marker lets the standby take the
  partitions at once.
- **Expiry missed entries stored in the same millisecond** (latent since M2; showed when the
  send path got faster): the age bound is now inclusive.

- **Found by the owner's review, fixed**: a key window could seal records before any standby
  held its keys; one standby per partition; failover after a crash took two lease periods;
  ownership never rebalanced (see above).
- **A stopped node still served requests on link connections opened before it stopped** (it
  could acknowledge a key-store copy and then exit). A stopped server now closes them.
- **A call to a node that had just closed its connection waited out the whole timeout (10 s)**
  instead of failing at once; the link client now notices the closed connection.
- **A standby that missed writes kept page files of keys the owner had deleted**, and a node
  that stopped being a standby kept its copy: both now deleted.
- **CI never ran the cluster or router tests against Valkey** (the compose job had no Valkey
  URL); they now run there, one at a time.

## Open risks and follow-ups

1. **Balance depends on partitions per node**: rendezvous hashing put 5/5/2 of twelve
   partitions on three nodes at the start of the run. Use several partitions per node (fixed
   when the topic is created); bounded-load placement (no node above its share) is a follow-up.
2. **Fewer live nodes than copies**: with one node left, writes continue on the one copy that
   exists (refusing would take the service down).
3. **Valkey without persistence** holds leases and routing: its loss costs a few seconds of
   re-subscribing and lease reclaiming, measured above. It needs a replica and Sentinel in
   deployment (Prompt 13).
4. **Clustered Valkey** (`SPUBLISH`/`SSUBSCRIBE`, per-shard subscribers) is designed, not built.
5. A graceful stop takes about 3 s to hand partitions over because client sessions drain
   first; handing over first would shorten it.
6. The single-machine numbers need the reference hardware (Prompt 13).
